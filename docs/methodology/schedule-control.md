# Schedule Control

**Status:** Methodology baseline  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-07

## Purpose

Schedule control compares actual and forecast progress against the approved schedule and identifies actions required to protect project objectives.

## Schedule Control Cycle

```text
Baseline
   ↓
Update
   ↓
Compare
   ↓
Analyze Variance
   ↓
Forecast
   ↓
Corrective Action
   ↓
Monitor
```

## Key Indicators

Potential indicators include:

- planned vs actual dates;
- milestone variance;
- critical-path movement;
- total float;
- near-critical activities;
- planned vs actual progress;
- forecast completion;
- schedule performance index where EVM is applicable.

## Variance Analysis

For each significant variance identify:

1. What changed?
2. Why did it change?
3. What is the impact?
4. What is the forecast?
5. What action is required?
6. Who owns the action?
7. By when?

## Recovery Planning

Recovery actions may include:

- resequencing;
- additional resources;
- productivity improvement;
- work-front changes;
- overtime/shift changes;
- procurement acceleration;
- approved scope or execution changes.

Recovery plans should be realistic and should not conceal the original variance.

## Schedule Change

Logic or date changes should be traceable to their reason and approval where required.
