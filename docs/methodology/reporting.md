# Project Controls Reporting

**Status:** Methodology baseline  
**Last updated:** 2026-10-07

## Purpose

Reporting converts project-controls data into information that supports management decisions.

## Reporting Levels

A reporting structure may include:

```text
Executive
   ↓
Project Management
   ↓
Project Controls
   ↓
Discipline / Work Package
```

## Core Report Content

A project-controls report may include:

- overall status;
- progress;
- schedule;
- cost;
- forecast;
- risks;
- changes;
- key milestones;
- major variances;
- corrective actions.

## Reporting Cycle

1. Freeze reporting data at the data date.
2. Validate schedule.
3. Reconcile cost.
4. Validate progress.
5. Update risk and change registers.
6. Calculate performance indicators.
7. Develop forecasts.
8. Review variances.
9. Prepare report.
10. Approve and distribute.

## Variance Reporting

A good variance narrative answers:

- What happened?
- Why?
- What is the impact?
- What is being done?
- What is the expected outcome?

## Report Integrity

Reports should clearly identify:

- reporting period;
- data date;
- baseline/version;
- assumptions;
- source data;
- calculation definitions.

## Management Focus

Reports should emphasize actionable information rather than simply presenting large volumes of data.
