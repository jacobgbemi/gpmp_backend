# GlintPM Private --- Problem Statement

## 1. Problem

Project-based organizations can lose time, money, and management
visibility when project information is fragmented across different
tools, files, teams, and processes.

The problem is not simply the absence of software.

The deeper problem is the absence of a reliable, connected system for
turning project information into timely and traceable control decisions.

## 2. Current-State Problems

### 2.1 Fragmented information

Project information may exist across:

-   Primavera P6
-   Microsoft Project
-   Excel
-   Cost systems
-   Emails
-   PDFs
-   Shared drives
-   Messaging platforms
-   Meeting notes
-   Individual team records

This fragmentation can make it difficult to establish a consistent view
of project status.

### 2.2 Manual reporting

Project teams may spend significant time:

-   Collecting information
-   Cleaning spreadsheets
-   Updating reports
-   Reconciling numbers
-   Preparing presentations
-   Chasing stakeholders for updates

This reduces the time available for analysis and proactive control.

### 2.3 Weak traceability

Management may see a number such as:

> Forecast cost = X

without being able to quickly trace it to:

-   Source data
-   Approved changes
-   Actual costs
-   Progress
-   Assumptions
-   Forecast calculations
-   Responsible users
-   Dates of changes

### 2.4 Reactive project control

When project information is not connected, problems may become visible
only after significant deviation has occurred.

### 2.5 Accountability gaps

A system may show that an issue exists without clearly establishing:

-   Who owns it
-   What action is required
-   When it is due
-   What decision is pending
-   Whether the action was completed

### 2.6 Lack of a common project-control workflow

Organizations may have experienced planners and project managers but
lack a consistent digital process for connecting:

**Plan → Baseline → Actual → Variance → Forecast → Risk → Action →
Decision.**

## 3. Consequences

Potential consequences include:

-   Delayed decision-making
-   Reporting inconsistencies
-   Excessive manual work
-   Poor visibility of emerging problems
-   Weak accountability
-   Difficulty reconstructing project history
-   Increased risk of cost and schedule overruns
-   Dependence on individual employees' spreadsheets and knowledge

These consequences should be validated with target customers rather than
assumed universally.

## 4. Problem Statement

> **Project-based organizations need a reliable way to connect project
> plans, progress, cost, risk, changes, reporting, and accountability so
> that management can understand what is happening, why it is happening,
> what is likely to happen next, and what action is required.**

## 5. Who Experiences the Problem?

Potential users include:

-   Project managers
-   Project controls managers
-   Planning/scheduling engineers
-   Cost engineers
-   Project controls engineers
-   Commercial teams
-   Project directors
-   Business owners/executives
-   PMO teams

## 6. Existing Alternatives

Organizations may currently use combinations of:

-   Primavera P6
-   Microsoft Project
-   Excel
-   Power BI
-   ERP/accounting systems
-   Document-management systems
-   Email
-   Messaging applications
-   Generic project-management software
-   Manual reporting processes

The product should not assume these alternatives are inadequate in every
organization. The intended opportunity is to connect project-control
workflows and information more effectively.

## 7. Problem Boundaries

GlintPM Private should not attempt to solve every project-management
problem in the first release.

The initial focus should remain on project controls, visibility,
reporting, accountability, and connected project information.

## 8. Validation Questions

Before major product expansion, validate:

1.  Which project-control problem costs customers the most?
2.  How is it currently handled?
3.  How frequently does it occur?
4.  What financial or operational consequences result?
5.  Who owns the problem internally?
6.  What systems are already used?
7.  What data is difficult to obtain?
8.  What would customers pay to improve?
9.  What prevents adoption of a new system?
10. Which workflow should be automated first?
